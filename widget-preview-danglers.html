AI TIP

Long Prompts Rot

Stuffing the context window doesn't make the model smarter. It makes it dumber, measurably.

Prompting desk·2 min

Chroma ran a proper technical report on what happens as you pile more tokens into a prompt, and the result should be taped to every keyboard: performance doesn't stay flat, it rots. Models handle short, focused context well, but as input grows, their ability to find and use the right detail degrades - even on tasks that look trivially easy. The million-token context window is storage, not attention. So the pro move isn't 'give it everything', it's 'give it exactly what this task needs'. Trim the brief. Pull the two relevant pages, not the whole book. If a prompt has grown into a novel, split the job into smaller calls. Bro, context is a budget, and every irrelevant token is spending the model's attention on junk.

The key points

  1. Bigger context isn't free: accuracy degrades as input length grows.

  2. Curate what goes in - two relevant pages beat the whole book.

  3. A prompt that became a novel should become several small calls.

Why it mattersWhen your agents start misbehaving, the bug is often a bloated prompt, not the model. Lean context is the cheapest accuracy upgrade you own.

DESIGN

Colour Has a Dress Code: 60-30-10

The oldest ratio in decorating quietly runs every clean interface you've ever admired.

Design desk·2 min

The 60-30-10 rule assigns every colour in a design a job and a budget: 60% dominant (your background, your base), 30% secondary (structure, supporting blocks), 10% accent (the thing you want eyes on). It's why restrained interfaces feel expensive and rainbow ones feel like a carnival. The accent only works because it's scarce - ten percent is a headline, fifty percent is noise. You're literally reading an example right now: this dangler is mostly indigo, structured in cream, and the orange name plate shows up exactly where you're supposed to look. The move to steal: pick your three colours first, assign the percentages, then refuse to let the accent leak. When everything shouts, nothing does.

The key points

  1. 60% dominant, 30% secondary, 10% accent - assign the jobs before you design.

  2. The accent's power is scarcity. Let it leak past 10% and the hierarchy collapses.

  3. Audit any design by asking which colour owns which share.

Why it mattersThree colours with assigned roles is a brand kit you can build tonight - for a channel, a deck or a poster - and it will instantly look more pro than a twelve-colour palette.
