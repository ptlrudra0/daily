A shared reading shelf

The Web Craft Library

A working research shelf on how great websites are made: composition and type, motion and texture, case-study lessons, tools and sources. Every entry links back to its origin so you can read the source yourself. Shared as a reading shelf for a friend learning the craft.

  1. 01
    Foundations

    Composition, grid, typography, color and contrast, Gestalt, accessibility - the base layer everything else sits on.

  2. 02
    Styles & Aesthetics

    A field guide to visual styles - brutalist to Y2K to editorial - with viewed examples, pitfalls and combination rules.

  3. 03
    Motion Craft

    Easing, duration, choreography, scroll systems, page transitions and micro-interactions, with the recipes behind them.

  4. 04
    Texture & Print

    Grain, paper, halftone, riso, ink-wash and material texture - static texture craft for web and editorial work.

  5. 05
    Case Studies

    How award-level sites were actually built - the decisions, the math, and the lessons, each linked to its write-up.

  6. 06
    People, Studios & Sources

    Studios, designers and developers worth following, galleries to mine, and curricula maps from public course outlines.

  7. 07
    Psychology & UX Reading

    Evidence-tagged UX research and book notes - what is known, what is claimed, and what is merely repeated.

  8. 08
    Building & Architecture

    Information architecture, navigation, layout primitives, design tokens, performance and shipping checklists.

  9. 09
    Tools & Assets

    Licence-checked fonts, icons, 3D, textures, animation libraries and templates - with the licence terms that matter.

  10. 10
    Anti-Slop

    The recognizable defaults of unconsidered design, and the direction-then-filter method for avoiding them.

  11. 11
    The Craft Pack

    A condensed starter: typography systems, grid and composition, colour, imagery and a final QA pass in one place.

Compiled from public articles, talks, docs and build write-ups. Recipes and short quotes are credited to their authors inline; this shelf adds organisation, not ownership. If a link has rotted, search the title - and when you use an asset, re-read its licence before shipping.
