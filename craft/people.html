Pattern licensing evidence and build decision

  • https://github.com/mike-hearn/transparent-textures describes adding transparency to Subtle Patterns. https://github.com/subtlepatterns/SubtlePatterns explicitly says CC BY-SA 3.0 and credits original authors. https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41515968 contains a post by the account mikehearn claiming authorship and CC BY 3.0. That account claim is not independent proof of relicensing rights. The two licences disagree; do not silently choose the less restrictive one. Prefer original procedural noise or assets with clear CC0 terms.
  • https://github.com/sschoger/hero-patterns read but supplies no licence in the fetched summary. https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=13677828 contains historical CC BY-SA 4.0 claims; current authoritative terms remain. A guessed LICENSE path returned 404, which does not establish that the repo lacks a licence.
  • Update: heropatterns.com homepage says "will always be free" but carries no licence text; both GitHub repos (steveschoger/hero-patterns, sschoger/hero-patterns) now 404; two independent secondary indexes claim CC BY 4.0. Verdict: treat patterns as attribution-required (credit Steve Schoger) if used at all, prefer procedural noise for anything commercial-critical.
  • Build guidance: save local assets rather than hotlinking third-party PNGs; preserve the attribution record and any share-alike obligations attached to modified assets. This is a reliability and rights precaution, not a claim that the whole website must use an asset's licence.

Community motion and 3D assets: shipping checklist

  • Rive: https://rive.app/docs/community/marketplace-overview and https://rive.app/docs/legal/terms-of-service establish the community/marketplace CC BY 4.0 grant. It covers remixing and modification, unlike a licence for the runtime alone. Keep the file URL, creator, licence URL and modification record in the build's asset ledger.
  • CC BY 4.0: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/ permits commercial sharing/adaptation with appropriate credit, licence link and indication of changes, without implying endorsement. Other rights (privacy, publicity, trademarks, etc.) can remain; a licence is not a guarantee that an uploader owns every included element.
  • Spline community: https://docs.spline.design/basics/community-platform says all community files are CC BY 4.0.: https://spline.design/terms also has broad restrictions on commercial exploitation of content not owned by the user, with a short Standard Licensing paragraph that does not state CC BY.: treat documentation as useful asset-licence evidence, but do not treat this as blanket clearance of every scene or third-party component. Recheck the selected scene and terms for a client build. Asset credit and paid watermark removal are separate issues; price note above remains the relevant export caveat.
  • Sketchfab: https://sketchfab.com/blogs/community/refine-downloadable-model-searches-with-new-license-filters/ explains CC0 (no conditions), CC BY (credit), NC (no commercial use), ND (no alteration), SA (same licence for derivatives) and editorial-only assets. Prefer CC0 for hero objects; reject NC for a client build and avoid ND when optimisation/changes are needed. Check actual item download availability and terms, not a collection title. This guide is historical and is not proof of today's search interface.
  • Sketchfab: https://sketchfab.com/features/free-3d-models now links royalty-free discovery to Fab. https://sketchfab.com/developers/download-api/guidelines requires account authentication for API downloads and attribution that follows assets in integrations. No specific CC0 model collection has been vetted here; do not claim an individual model is ready to use.

Build-time tool recipes and export costs

  • Rive: https://rive.app/docs/editor/exporting/exporting-for-runtime states runtime .riv export is paid. https://rive.app/pricing currently advertises $9/month to ship; https://rive.app/blog/rive-s-new-9-mo-plan explains Cadet is $9/month billed annually or $17/month billed monthly, with unlimited exports and up to 3 seats. Recheck exact checkout total and billing term before any purchase. Free editor access and CC BY marketplace rights do not grant free custom export. The announcement explicitly allows running Marketplace/docs demos without exporting; existing exports do not require an active subscription. Paid unlock to consider only if we need new/modified .riv exports, not a requirement to keep learning.
  • Open Props: https://open-props.style/ is MIT and gives npm/CSS imports plus individual packs (sizes, easings, animations). Import tokens selectively; normalize and styled buttons are optional. Suggested approach: use sizes/easing tokens behind brand-specific aliases, leave default cards, gradients and colours out unless they fit the brief. Pin the package/version for a real build rather than relying on an unversioned CDN. This avoids importing someone else's visual style along with useful primitives.
  • cubic-bezier: https://cubic-bezier.com/ offers preview/compare, duration adjustment and curve library import/export. Source licence read: https://github.com/LeaVerou/cubic-bezier/blob/gh-pages/LICENSE, MIT, retain copyright/permission when copying tool code. Curve choice is a design decision, not a reason to include the whole tool in a website.
  • Utopia: https://utopia.fyi/ describes interpolating type and spacing between small and large viewport scales, with free tools and no framework dependency. https://github.com/trys/utopia-core documents JS/TS calculateTypeScale, calculateSpaceScale, calculateClamp and container-relative options.: https://github.com/trys/utopia-core/blob/main/package.json declares ISC.: the attempted LICENSE path returned 404, so preserve/recheck the licence notice in the package before vendoring code. Generated CSS and source-package reuse are separate questions. Fluid typography does not remove the need for layout breakpoints or testing text zoom/wrapping; that is an implementation caution.

Codrops free templates: Isle Thorne and Wide

Sources: tympanus.net/codrops/2026/10/07/isle-thorne-collective-astro-template/, tympanus.net/codrops/2026/09/28/wide-template/. Both MIT/free. - ISLE THORNE COLLECTIVE (free Astro furniture-shop template, design by Elena Smirnova, by Manoela Ilic): a REFERENCE IMPLEMENTATION of progressive enhancement. Static on purpose: no UI framework/CMS/backend, products are Markdown files, cart in localStorage, checkout/newsletter are demos - "it isn't a complete shop" said up front, README maps where payments/accounts/stock would connect. ONE grid utility (grid-site: 2 cols on phones, 12 from 1024px) places every page's content - "navigation, piece counts and article text all start on the same column." Instrument Sans self-hosted via Astro Fonts API with a metric-adjusted fallback so text doesn't shift on load. PLAIN HTML WHERE IT CAN BE: photo rows are Embla Carousel but still scroll sideways without JS; product panels are <details> sharing a name (browser keeps one open); cart + newsletter are <dialog> elements (which the browser draws ABOVE the page transition - a feature to design around, not fight). Pages and links work without JS entirely; Lenis off under reduced motion. Content = Astro content collections (one JSON + one Markdown per product/story, filename = URL); pages read through two small helpers so a CMS loader can replace collections without touching pages. Transitions: Astro client router + Interlude (21 transitions included, swap via one config key); scripts run once per VISIT so per-page setup lives in Interlude page hooks; transition cost honestly stated: ~39 kB compressed on top of ~18 kB. npm create astro@latest -- --template codrops/isle-thorne-collective. - WIDE by BL/S (open-source grid experiment + optional WebGL shader): the ANTI-EFFECT MANIFESTO - "how much can you get from a simple visual system before adding another effect just because you can? Sometimes a grid is enough. Sometimes typography doing the right thing is enough. And sometimes someone says: let's add a shader." TEN CARDS, FIVE COMPOSITIONS: cards created in the DOM ONCE (ITEMS array) and never recreated - "compositions only change their position and size", which is what makes continuous rearrangement cheap. Layouts described as COLUMNS not coordinates: a number = fixed vw, 'ratio' = from the card's proportion, 'eq'/'auto' share what is left. The MIRRORED state is never authored - derived by reversing the column array (typography mirror = a clone with sides flipped). No giant controller: geometry computes positions, one dispatcher handles movement inside a composition, another handles mirror-switching - "each system has its own job. Which becomes particularly important when things start moving." The word "Wide" is the anchor that keeps every reorganisation feeling like one visual system (cf. pixel-as-motif). - TUTORIALS TAG MAP (8 entries): ghost trails, custom cursor trail, parallel page transitions, pixel transition+reveal, Atelier UI WebGL/DOM sync, libvips compression tool, Isle Thorne, Wide. The tag forms a curriculum: interaction texture -> transition architecture -> templates that package both.

SPOTLIGHT VEIN SYNTHESIS - 22 creative careers, what the patterns say

Built from codrops designer+developer spotlights, [all V full reads]. 1. NOBODY STARTED WHERE THEY ENDED. Painter (Papadopoulou), philosophy student (Bernadou), embedded electronics (Kondrashova), developer-who-thought-design-wasn't-for-her (Tykhonova), C++ gamedev dreamer (Penev), YouTube GFX banner maker (Norman). The wandering path is the norm, not the exception. Each detour becomes a differentiator later (Penev's engine-building -> StringTune; Vargas's gaming -> PlayStation). 2. THE BORDER BETWEEN DESIGN AND DEV IS THE JOB. Every spotlight sits on it from some side: designers who learned to build (Tkachev, Gilroy, Brouwer), developers who learned to see (Vestergaard, Penev), people who refuse the wall ("code is just a creative medium" - Vanderpotte). Garden Eight and mount inc. made the same point at studio scale. 3. BURNOUT HAS A PRECISE ADDRESS: THE HANDOFF. Gilroy's Figma dying in other people's builds; Bernadou's "once the design is done, what comes next?" frustration; Lunardi's "half-finished work". The cure in every case: move toward the other side of the handoff. 4. UNPAID EXPERIMENTS ARE THE CAREER ENGINE. Kondrashova's silly demos -> CodePen Most Hearted -> commercial gigs. Bernadou's experiment diary -> SOTD portfolio -> first job. Penev's library. Vanderpotte: "build things for no reason." Studio-scale echo: OFF+BRAND's Totem loss-leader, Studio Freight incubating Lenis. The pattern is unanimous. 5. COMMUNITY IS THE CLASSROOM. CodePen/X sharing (Kondrashova, Bernadou), GFX clans (Norman), OkayDev (Adolfsson, Lunardi), Codrops itself named as teacher by ~10 of them. Formal education appears in barely half. 6. NOTEBOOK BEFORE KEYBOARD. Perrey, Bernadou, and others sketch by hand first - "typing my ideas has always felt like a way to lose them." 7. SIMPLICITY IS A SENIOR MOVE. Veterans remove machinery: Vestergaard's PNG frames over WebGL sprites, Penev's SVG over canvas, Tkachev's 80% removal portfolio, Lunardi's "removing code is the real work." Juniors add; seniors subtract. 8. ON AI (the 2026 mood, stated across essays): not replacement, not threat - "I am the director of everything that ends up in the codebase" (Lunardi); "the shackles are off" for solo execution (Gilroy); "craft matters more, not less - taste and judgment take years and no model does that work for you" (Lunardi); friction-as-a-feature essay (Kowalski) is the same argument from the design side.
