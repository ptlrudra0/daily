Design tokens

W3C Design Tokens Community Group format, Final Community Group Report 28 Oct 2025 (not a W3C Standard, stable, intended for implementation). Tokens = indivisible design-system pieces (colour, spacing, type scale). Use a tokens JSON (or CSS variables) as the single source for values so every page reuses them. Tools such as Style Dictionary transform tokens to CSS.

Free assets and their terms (checked Oct 6 2026)

  • Poly Haven (polyhaven.com): HDRIs, textures, 3D models, all CC0 (public domain), API at polyhaven.com/our-api. Site ToS still bars scraping/misuse of the website itself; download via their API/downloads.
  • Fontshare (fontshare.com): all fonts free for personal and commercial use, ITF Free Font License (fontshare.com/licenses/itf-ffl). Some closed-source freeware, some open source. Good for distinctive display faces beyond Google Fonts.
  • Photos: Pexels (pexels.com/license: free, no attribution needed), Unsplash, Pixabay all allow commercial use; read each licence, no resale of unaltered files, caution with identifiable people/trademarks (see freecreativeassets.com/guides/free-stock-photo-licenses).
  • 3D: Sketchfab has free models under mixed licences (CC-BY needs attribution), check each. Spline (spline.design) 3D embeds; web export watermark depends on plan (spline.design/pricing).

  • Animation: Lottie (LottieFiles free animations, per-animation licence), Rive (rive.app/marketplace, rive-app/rive-lottie). Rive vs Lottie: lottie-web ~250KB (60KB gz) per johnson.works/blog/lottie-vs-rive-animations; Rive runtime smaller. Prefer Rive/CSS/SVG for hero motion. Rule: log licence + URL next to every asset used in a build.

  • Icons (verified licences Oct 6): Lucide (github.com/lucide-icons/lucide) ISC, free for commercial use; Phosphor (github.com/phosphor-icons/core) MIT, six weights as SVG.

Unsplash

Licence (quoted from unsplash.com/terms section 5, which quotes the licence; unsplash.com/license itself returned HTTP 401 to my fetcher): "an irrevocable, nonexclusive, worldwide copyright license to download, copy, modify, distribute, perform, and use images from Unsplash for free, including for commercial purposes, without permission from or attributing the photographer or Unsplash, but this license does not include the right to compile images from Unsplash to replicate a similar or competing service." Not covered (terms s.5): trademarks, logos or brands visible in images; recognisable people (may need their permission); works of art or authorship shown in images. Check before using any photo with a face, a logo, a product brand, or artwork. Attribution: not required, appreciated. Good practice: credit in the site footer or an image-credits page. Reselling: redistributing, selling or printing and selling images downloaded from Unsplash violates the licence/ToS; API rules also bar selling unaltered photos (prints, products). "Compiling" means building a competing stock site or mass scraping. Using an unaltered photo as a hero on a client website is allowed; selling the file itself is not. Unsplash+ is a separate paid product with its own licence (unsplash.com/plus/license, not read). Free licence applies to the free library only. Practical rules: download from the site (not mass-scrape), keep a credits log (photographer, URL, date), re-check the licence page in a browser if the project is commercial since terms can change (terms page read Oct 6 2026).

Picking imagery that does not look like generic stock [analysis + NN/g photos-as-web-content + Della Mattia luxury article]

  • Evidence: users ignore decorative stock and examine real people, real products and information-carrying images (NN/g). Luxury readers recognise stock instantly (Della Mattia).
  • Choose images that carry specific information or a single mood tied to the project's subject, not "people smiling at laptop", handshakes, generic skylines, or abstract gradients.
  • Prefer: texture/material close-ups, architecture, landscape, still life, hands at work, odd crops, off-centre subjects with natural light. Avoid: posed models, centred subjects, over-saturated HDR, cliché hero concepts.
  • Search with specific nouns and moods (e.g. "concrete stairwell shadow", "salt flat dusk") rather than category words ("business", "technology"); scroll past page 1-2 since the top results are the most reused.
  • Make a set look like one shoot: same light direction, colour temperature and subject distance; apply one grade (black-and-white or a single tint) to unify mixed sources; crop deliberately to the composition (rule of thirds).
  • Mix sources: his own stills, generated art, Poly Haven CC0 3D/HDRI, then Unsplash as the minority.
  • Optimise: AVIF/WebP, sized to layout, explicit width/height, lazy-load below the fold, preload the hero.

Coolors palette generator (source)

What it does: press spacebar to generate palettes, explore millions of user palettes, extract colours from an image, check accessibility contrast, preview palettes on real designs, organise into projects, export in several formats; works on web, mobile apps and plugins (home page text, via search result). Free plan: "always free", no time limit; limits on number of saved palettes/projects/collections (delete old ones to save new). Free includes ads. Pro adds: Coolors AI (credits), no ads, palettes up to 10 colours (free is 5), 10M+ ready schemes, unlimited saved palettes/colours/projects, pick colours from image, collages, pro profile, contrast check of palette variations, palette-visualizer layouts, advanced export (Web, UI, Print, Motion formats), custom logo on exported PDFs, private profile. Licence for palettes: colour values are not copyrightable material; check Terms if redistributing a collection. Use: seed from the brand's anchor colour, then check WCAG contrast (see , Radix 12-step scales for UI ramps); his champagne-gold accent can be tested here against pure black.

Slidesgo (source)

Presentation templates for Google Slides, PowerPoint (and Keynote infographics). Licence: free for personal AND commercial use; you may not sublicense, sell, rent or redistribute Slidesgo content (or modified versions) without authorisation. Free users must attribute Slidesgo by keeping the credits slide; this also applies when using their infographics in a deck not built from a template. Premium lets you share without crediting Slidesgo and removes ads (15,000+ premium assets per their teacher page). Limits: unregistered users cannot download; registered free users get 3 template downloads and 3 AI-generated presentations per month; Premium unlimited, capped at 150 per month for security (pricing page). Re-downloads, the other app format and colour variants of an already-downloaded template do not count. Fonts: Google Slides pulls Google Fonts automatically; PowerPoint may need them installed. Terms page: https://slidesgo.com/terms-conditions (section 7.4 attribution; not read in full). For decks: pick one template, replace its fonts and palette with the brand's, edit the master slides (Slide > Edit master / View > Slide Master) rather than slides, remove template decoration, keep to one idea per slide. Free tier: leave the credits slide.

Attribution (verbatim gist): "Unless otherwise indicated, authorization to use Magnific Content is free of charge and conditioned upon any use... being duly attributed to the Website/Company and, where applicable, to the Collaborator." To use it without attribution you must buy a Premium, Premium+ or Pro subscription and download the content while subscribed. Other: Sponsored and Collaborators content can carry its own conditions; some content is under a separate "Specific License" shown on the page; licensed fonts allow website use under section 8.2 with restrictions (no embedding that exposes the font files, no modification). AI-generated "Originals" are view-only. Liability cap: EUR 100 on free or Essential plans, EUR 10,000 Premium, EUR 50,000 Premium+/Pro. Re-check the page before each commercial project; the product was renamed recently so terms may move. Practical: for a client site use the premium download (no credit needed) or credit it in the footer; prefer vectors that you recolour so they do not look like stock.

Interactive 3D/animation tools — pricing verified Oct 12

  • Spline: Free = web exports WITH watermark; Hobby $15/mo ($12 annual) removes watermark + higher-res images; Pro $30/mo ($25 annual) adds Apple/Android exports, video export, unlimited Variables/APIs/Webhooks; Max $70/mo ($60 annual) mainly more AI credits. Per-seat USD; yearly ~14-20% off.
  • Rive: "Free to create, $9/mo to ship" — Free = 3 collaborative files in the editor; Cadet $9/mo (3 seats) ships to apps/products/games with unlimited files; Voyager $32/mo (25 seats, Libraries); Enterprise $120/mo ($10M+ revenue orgs, SOC2, dedicated support). Updates Oct 7 note: Cadet pricing confirmed current.
  • Choice rule: Spline = 3D scenes/exports to web (watermark on free), Rive = interactive state-machine animation shipped to runtimes (free until you ship). See [[case-studies]] 2Advanced Rive rearchitecture.
